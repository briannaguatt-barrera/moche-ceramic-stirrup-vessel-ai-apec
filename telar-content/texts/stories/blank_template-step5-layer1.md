Moche culture is strongly rooted in religion. The Moche people's everyday lifestyle was influenced by religious beliefs, specifically beliefs concerning the cycle of life and death. It is important to first note that Ai Apaec portrayed the main creator of the universe and god of the sky. Additionally, he was associated with three worlds: the sky, earth, and underworld. He was one of many gods that the Moche worshiped and therefore symbolically represented as objects. This ceramic stirrup vessel which is referred to as corn cob, was spiritually used in ceremonies and burial traditions. Since their culture was also heavily rooted in agriculture, corn was a way of connecting the world of the living with the divine. Those of high status, often rulers, requested that the vessel should travel with them to the afterlife by being buried in the tombs alongside them. This was seen as a way to carry and keep power and protection forever. The act of bringing it through worlds demonstrates how deeply intertwined the Moche are with the spiritual world.
