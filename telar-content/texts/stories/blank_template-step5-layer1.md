Moche culture is strongly rooted in religion. They have ceremonial practices established with the intent of connecting those of the living with the dead. This ceramic stirrup vessel of the Ai Apaec, commonly referred to as corn cob, was spiritually used in ceremonies and burial traditions. It is important to first note that Ai Apaec portrayed the main creator of the universe and god of the sky. Since Moche culture was also heavily rooted in agriculture, corn was a way of connecting the world of the living with the divine. Those of high status, often rulers, requested that the vessel should travel with them to the afterlife by being buried in the tombs alongside them. Bringing objects such as the Ai Apaec vessel  through various spiritual worlds demonstrates how death does not symbolize the end, but rather acts as a transition into another phase of existence.
