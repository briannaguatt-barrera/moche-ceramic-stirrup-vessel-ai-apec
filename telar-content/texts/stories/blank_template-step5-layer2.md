The Moche people's beliefs regarding the supernatural shaped their everyday lifestyle. This relationship is highlighted in Moche art and sacred observances through the Ai Apaec. The Ai Apaec was a mythical being that was associated with three worlds: the sky, earth, and underworld. He was one of many gods that the Moche worshiped and therefore symbolically represented through objects. His appearance on ceremonial objects, including this ceramic stirrup-spout vessel, demonstrates that these creations were made with a greater purpose beyond physicality. This corn-cob shaped vessel reflects the religious and agricultural significance of corn in Moche culture. It is crucial to highlight that corn was an essential crop to Moche people and was used in harvests in order to survive. As a result, they often prayed to divine forces in order to bring great prosperity to their land, such as an abundance of corn. In addition to economic stability, elite members of society often asked that the Ai Apaec vessel be buried alongside them in order to continue the streak of good fortune into the afterlife. Not only did this tradition illustrate the Moche people's view in life after death, but also explains the role that religious devotion played in preserving status, wealth, and protection.
