The vessel was made separately from its spout and was formed using a 2-piece mold made of fired clay. The clay was poured into the mold and later completely attached using a coiling method. The spout was made in a very similar way resulting in two long tubes that were bent to create the hoop of the stirrup, careful to use very moist clay as it could easily break. After attaching the spout and smoothing with leather the vessel was painted with the white paint. A final polishing was done with a hard rock after it was thoroughly dry. Then it would finally be fired in an open pit with various other vessels stacked on top of each other, the oxidizing environment would create the red color of the ceramic. Little is known about its use besides that it was a religious good that was buried alongside dead ruler. It is likely that the vessel could have held a fermented corn drink (Chicha) and been used in religious gatherings, as a monument to the god of the harvest or fertility.**
